🔍 Reading the number critically

The calculator was built for India, where each kWh of grid electricity emits roughly 0.7 kg CO₂ (mostly coal). Nepal’s grid is almost entirely hydropower — about 0.02–0.03 kg per kWh across its life cycle, with some imports from India in the dry season. So my 0.5 t “Energy” is likely an overestimate. My real levers are even more clearly food, cooking fuel (100% imported LPG) and consumption.

🧭 What this told me about my lifestyle

I expected transport to dominate. It is only 11%. The things I never thought of as “climate decisions” — dal or meat, a pressure cooker or an open pot, a plastic bag or a jhola — are where most of my footprint sits. Nepal’s per-person average is tiny, but an urban, middle-class lifestyle in Kathmandu is not: my number sits above the global average.

Method notes & emission factors
  • Meat-free day: ≈ 1.5 kg CO₂e saved per meat meal replaced by pulses — a weighted mix of chicken, buff and mutton servings (~100 g) vs. dal, from Poore & Nemecek (2018) global medians.
  • Walk / bus: ≈ 0.06 kg CO₂e per km avoided on a petrol motorbike / ride-hail (fuel ~45 km/L × 2.3 kg CO₂/L, plus vehicle life cycle); walking ≈ 0, electric bus ≈ 0 on hydropower.
  • Electricity: 0.72 kg CO₂/kWh — the Indian grid factor the calculator uses. On Nepal’s hydro grid the real saving is far smaller.
  • LPG: one 14.2 kg cylinder ≈ 42 kg CO₂ (2.98 kg CO₂/kg LPG).
  • Food waste: ≈ 2.5 kg CO₂e per kg of food not wasted (FAO, 2013 global wastage footprint ÷ wasted mass).
  • Plastic: ≈ 0.05 kg CO₂e per bag/bottle avoided — deliberately shown to prove a point: plastic matters for land and rivers far more than for carbon.

Step 4 Think beyond carbon

My ecological footprint is bigger than my carbon number

A carbon footprint counts gases. An ecological footprint counts the land and sea area — cropland, grazing land, forests, fishing grounds, built-up land — needed to supply what I consume and absorb what I waste (Wackernagel & Rees, 1996). Two of my everyday choices connect directly to it:

1.7

Earths — roughly how many humanity currently uses each year, by Global Footprint Network estimates. We are drawing down nature’s capital, not living on its interest.

<1%

of my carbon footprint is saved by refusing ~15 plastic items a week (≈ 39 kg CO₂e/yr) — yet plastic is one of my most visible pressures on land and rivers. What we don’t measure, we don’t manage.
